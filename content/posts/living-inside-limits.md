My body has limits. They are not always visible, predictable, or convenient. They do not disappear because a day matters, because someone needs something, or because I want badly enough to participate.

Living with chronic illness means learning that ability is not a stable number. The distance between what I want to do and what my body can do changes. Planning can become negotiation. Rest can become necessary long before it feels earned.

## The cost people do not see

A finished task does not show what it cost. Showing up does not mean showing up was easy. A good hour does not disprove a difficult week.

There is work in measuring energy, anticipating symptoms, changing plans, recovering, and deciding which needs are allowed to become urgent. Much of that work happens privately. From the outside, a limit can look like reluctance or inconsistency. From inside the body, it may be the most responsible choice available.

I am still learning to respect that distinction.

## A life that includes the body

Acceptance is not the same as being pleased. It does not mean giving up on care, change, treatment, or possibility. It means building from the body I actually live in instead of constantly punishing it for failing to become an imagined one.

There can be grief in that. There can also be precision, adaptation, humor, anger, and tenderness.

I do not want every story about limitation to end with triumph. Some days the honest ending is that something was hard and remained hard. A life can still be meaningful without defeating every boundary inside it.
