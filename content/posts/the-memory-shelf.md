Losing my mom changed the weight of memory. A detail can feel like a gift and an injury at the same time: proof that something was real, and proof that it cannot happen again in the same way.

I do not want to remember only in milestones. The stories that matter are not always the ones that fit neatly into a timeline. Sometimes memory is a room, a sound, an expression, an object, or a sentence whose importance only became visible later.

## Why a shelf

A shelf does not force its contents into a single story. Things can sit beside one another without matching. A good memory does not cancel a painful one. A painful memory does not erase love. Grief can keep them all close, even when their edges do not align.

This part of the archive will hold what I am ready to preserve about my mom: stories, letters, remembered objects, fragments of good days, and the parts that still hurt. I may return to the same memory more than once and understand it differently each time.

That is not inconsistency. It is what living memory does.

## What I am trying to keep

Grief can make remembering feel urgent. There is a fear that small details will disappear if they are not written down quickly enough. But memory also has its own pace. I do not need to recover everything at once for what remains to matter.

The shelf can grow slowly. Some spaces may stay empty. Some stories may remain private.

What is placed here is not a complete portrait of my mom or of our relationship. It is a way to keep contact with particular moments—to say: this happened, this mattered, and I am still carrying it.
