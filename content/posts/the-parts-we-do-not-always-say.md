There are experiences that are difficult to describe while they are happening. Language can feel too small, too clinical, too dramatic, or too dangerous. Silence is not always secrecy. Sometimes it is the only container available until something safer can be built.

For people who know me, parts of this archive may feel like new information. I understand that. But the first time a story is heard is not necessarily the first time it existed.

## Why telling takes time

Mental health struggles can change how clearly a person can narrate their own life. Sometimes survival comes before explanation. Sometimes shame edits the story before it reaches anyone else. Sometimes we protect the people we love from truths we fear will hurt them, frighten them, or change how they see us.

And sometimes there is simply no ordinary opening in a conversation for the full answer.

Writing gives me a different kind of time. I can begin, stop, revise, wait, and decide whether the page should remain private. I can name something without having to manage another person's reaction in the same moment.

## A page is not an emergency

If I publish a difficult experience here, it means I chose to make that piece public. It does not necessarily mean the experience is happening now. It does not ask anyone to panic, diagnose, or take control away from me.

Care can begin with listening. Concern can coexist with respect. A story can be serious without becoming a request for someone else to author its ending.

I want this archive to make room for truth at the pace truth becomes possible.
